2026-02-01, 2026-05-01, 2026-08-01, and 2026-11-01 at 00Z. The same four dates

## Workflow

One self-contained notebook per initial-condition method. Each runs the whole
workflow for its method: read the model (and observed) key fields, choose the
box size (leave-one-out test, see "Choice of box size"), select or build the
initial conditions, write the restarts, evaluate them against the actual 2015
restarts, draw the native-mesh maps, and save its outputs for the comparison
notebook. Every option (dates, candidate years, climatology years, box test,
mode, weights, observations, anomaly settings, output switches) is set in the
notebook's configuration cell. The land, river, and sea-ice restarts of the
BruteForce archive are the JRA55-FOSIRL restarts, so the figures label every
archive state, including the 2015 reference, as JRA55-FOSIRL (`IC_SOURCE`).

| Notebook | Method | Restarts written to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `01_clim_match_ic.ipynb` | climatology-match: the archive year closest to the model climatology | `YYYYMMDD/{lnd,rof,ice,cpl}/clim/` |
| `02_obs_match_ic.ipynb` | observation-match: the archive year closest to the observed anomaly (`OBS_SET`) | `YYYYMMDD/{lnd,rof,ice,cpl}/obs/` |

## Land, river, and sea-ice states from a selected year

No model restarts exist for 2026 (the BruteForce set ends in 2018). Averaging
restarts across years breaks the internal consistency of the model state
(snow layers, ice categories, phenology), so for each target date one year of
the BruteForce archive is selected and its ELM, MOSART, and MPAS-Seaice
restarts are used unchanged (renamed to the target date). The branches choose
the year:

| Branch | Selected year | Target | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `clim` | closest to the climatology | mean of the climatology years | `initial_conditions/YYYYMMDD/<comp>/clim/` |
| `obs` | closest to the observed state | observed anomaly on the target date: ERA5-Land (land) and NOAA/NSIDC CDR (sea ice) by default (`OBS_SET = "era5land_nsidc"`), or ERA5 for both (`OBS_SET = "era5"`) | `initial_conditions/YYYYMMDD/<comp>/obs/` |

The observation set used is recorded in `selection.json` (the plan's
observation roots and fields) and in the notebook's saved `config.json`; a
run with another `OBS_SET` replaces the `obs/` restarts.

`hybrid_ic` (`03_hybrid_ic.ipynb`) instead adjusts the `clim` year
to the model climatology plus the observed (ERA5-Land/NSIDC) anomaly. It is on
hold (see "Climatology plus observed anomaly").

The metric below is written with ERA5 variable names; ERA5-Land uses the same
names (`sd`, `swvl1`-`swvl4`, `stl1`-`stl4`) on a 1-degree grid, and the NSIDC CDR
gives `ci` as a daily mean. The archive
`/global/cfs/cdirs/e3sm/zhan391/v3.LR.S2D.ENSINT/BruteForce/`, ERA5
(`/global/cfs/projectdirs/m3522/cmip6/ERA5`), ERA5-Land, and NOAA/NSIDC
(`/global/cfs/cdirs/e3sm/zhan391/data/{ERA5_LND,NOAA_NSIDC}`) are only read.
Candidate years are those with all three restarts for the target calendar date
from `SEARCH_FIRST_YEAR` = 1995 to `SEARCH_LAST_YEAR` (2018), excluding the target
year itself. The climatology (the mean and interannual spread that standardize
every model and observed field) uses the years `CLIM_FIRST_YEAR`-`CLIM_LAST_YEAR`;
by default these are the candidate years, and the target year is left out of
both (`EXCLUDE_TARGET_YEAR`).

### Distance metric

1. **Key fields.** One value per model point, with an area weight `w_i`:

   | Component | Field | Model definition | Weight | ERA5 (`obs`) |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Sea ice | NH and SH ice concentration | sum of `iceAreaCategory` | `areaCell`, per hemisphere | `ci` |
   | Sea ice | NH and SH ice thickness | sum of `iceVolumeCategory` (m) | as above | -- |
   | Sea ice | NH and SH snow on ice | sum of `snowVolumeCategory` (m) | as above | -- |
   | Land | snow water equivalent | `H2OSNO` (mm) | `cols1d_wtxy * cos(lat)`, active columns | `sd` |
   | Land | surface soil water | `H2OSOI_LIQ + H2OSOI_ICE`, soil layers 1-3 (about 0-9 cm) | as above | `swvl1` (0-7 cm) |
   | Land | subsurface soil water | `H2OSOI_LIQ + H2OSOI_ICE`, soil layers 4 and below | as above | `swvl2-4`, depth-weighted (7-289 cm) |
   | Land | surface soil temperature | `T_SOISNO`, soil layer 1 (K) | as above | `stl1` |
   | Land | subsurface soil temperature | `T_SOISNO`, soil layers 4-10, depth-weighted over 0.09-2.89 m (K) | as above | `stl2-4`, depth-weighted (7-289 cm) |

   The two soil-water fields cover disjoint layers, as do the two
   soil-temperature fields (layer 1, and layers 4-10), so no layer is counted
   twice. The ELM restart does not store layer depths; the standard 15-layer
   ELM soil grid is used (nodes `z_j = 0.025 (exp(0.5 (j - 0.5)) - 1)` m),
   with layer 10 counted only above 2.89 m, the bottom of ERA5 `stl4`. The river (MOSART) is not part of the distance: its channel storage
   is driven by land runoff and would count land twice. Its restart is still
   written, always from the land year.

   ERA5 is taken at 00Z of the target date (and of the same date in each
   candidate year), at the nearest 0.25-degree point to each sea-ice cell or
   0.5-degree land grid cell (land columns are averaged to grid cells for the
   comparison). Points missing in any year get zero weight.

2. **Standardized anomalies.** For each field, with `xbar_i` and `s_i^2` the
   mean and interannual variance at point `i` over the climatology years
   (by default the candidate years):

   ```text
   z_yi = (x_yi - xbar_i) / S,    S^2 = sum_i w_i s_i^2 / sum_i w_i
   ```

3. **Field distance** from a target `t`:

   ```text
   d_f(y) = sqrt( sum_i w_i (z_yi - t_i)^2 / sum_i w_i )
   ```

   - `clim`: `t = 0`. `d_f` is the area-weighted RMS anomaly divided by the
     area-weighted RMS interannual spread; when the climatology years are the
     candidate years, its mean square over the candidates is exactly 1, so
     `d_f < 1` is closer than a typical year.
   - `obs`: `t` is the ERA5 standardized anomaly on the target date,
     `(o_T - obar) / S_obs`, with the ERA5 mean and spread over the same
     climatology years and date. Anomalies are compared, so model-ERA5 biases and
     unit or layer-depth differences cancel. Only fields with an ERA5
     counterpart are used.

   With `box = (dlat, dlon)` (default 15 x 30 degrees, distances averaged over 4
   placements of the box edges), model and observed values are first averaged
   over lat-lon boxes with the area weights, so years are
   matched on large-scale anomalies instead of grid-point detail (see "Choice
   of box size").

4. **Component distance.** `D_c(y) = sqrt(mean of d_f(y)^2 over the component's fields)`.

5. **Joint distance.** `D(y) = sqrt(sum_c a_c D_c(y)^2 / sum_c a_c)`, with
   component weights `a_c` (default 1 each).

6. **Selection.** By default (`joint`) the year with the smallest `D`
   supplies land, river, and sea ice, so all three come from the same coupled
   state. `per_component` uses each component's smallest `D_c` for land and
   sea ice. The river always takes the land year.

The `obs*` branches need observations for the target date: ERA5-Land and NSIDC
on disk reach 2026-08-01 (2026-11-01 waits for them); ERA5 ends in June 2026,
so `obs` also waits for 2026-08-01.

### Choice of box size

The box size is chosen first (section 2 of each workflow notebook), from the archive
alone, so the 2015 test dates stay independent of it. For each calendar date
and each year `y` of 1995-2018, `y` is treated as the unknown target: the `obs`
selection picks, from the other years, the year closest to the observed
(ERA5-Land/NSIDC) anomaly of `y`, and the pick is scored against the actual model state of `y`, on the
native model grid (primary criterion) and as 30 x 60 box means (tie-breaker).
Every box is judged at these same two scales. Skill is 0 for a random year and
1 for the closest year; 24 test years per date, mean over the four dates
(ERA5-Land and NOAA/NSIDC observations; five land fields including subsurface
soil temperature; box distances averaged over 4 placements of the box edges,
see below):

| `box` | Grid points, observable fields | Grid points, all key fields | 30 x 60 means, observable | 30 x 60 means, all key fields |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| model points | 0.798 | 0.677 | 0.775 | 0.652 |
| 10 x 20 | **0.801** | **0.715** | 0.772 | 0.676 |
| 15 x 30 | 0.780 | 0.691 | 0.795 | **0.690** |
| 20 x 40 | 0.764 | 0.695 | **0.800** | 0.673 |
| 30 x 60 | 0.709 | 0.586 | **0.800** | 0.631 |
| 45 x 90 | 0.669 | 0.633 | 0.773 | 0.678 |

The primary criterion is closeness on the native model grid (the first two
columns): grid points, 10 x 20, and 15 x 30 are tied there (within two standard
errors of the best), while larger boxes are clearly worse. Among the tied
boxes, 15 x 30 matches the large-scale pattern best (30 x 60 box means), so it
is chosen. Month-to-month differences are mostly within two standard errors,
so one box is used for all dates.

**Box-edge placement.** With a single grid of boxes, the result depended on
where the box edges fell (15 x 30 scored below both 10 x 20 and 20 x 40). The
selection distance is therefore the RMS over 4 placements of the box grid
(standard, and shifted by half a box in latitude, in longitude, and in both;
`box_shifts=4` in `compute_distances`, `--box-shifts` in the CLI). This
removes the edge noise and improves the selection for every box size. The
default is `box = (15, 30)`, `box_shifts = 4` for both branches. (An earlier
choice of 30 x 60 was based on the four 2015 dates alone, scored at the
selection box, with 1980-2018 candidates.)

### Climatology plus observed anomaly (`hybrid_ic`)

**On hold** (2026-10-23) for further exploration of the deep-soil treatment
(the open points below). Its restarts for the seven dates stay in
`YYYYMMDD/{lnd,rof,ice,cpl}/hybrid_ic/` for comparison, but are not used for
hindcasts; `../../hindcast_run/RTENSO2026/hybrid_ic/` is not run.

`util/anom_restart.py` (notebook 03) starts from the `clim` restart and moves
its key fields to the model climatology plus the observed anomaly, point by
point: the observed standardized anomaly (ERA5-Land, NSIDC) is given the
model's own interannual spread (`scale="point"`), capped at 3 model standard
deviations (`z_max`), and applied as a ratio (snow, soil water, sea ice;
0.25-4x) or a shift (soil temperature), kept within the range of the archive
years at each point (`envelope`). Only soil and crop columns change; the
water table, aquifer, river, and coupler keep the base state.

Deep soil. ERA5-Land soil ends at 2.89 m (`stl4`/`swvl4`); ELM has 15 layers
to 42 m, with water only to the bedrock (layers 1-10, to 3.8 m, in these
restarts):

- Soil water: the factor varies linearly in depth from its surface value
  (centre of layers 1-3) to a deep value at 1.49 m, solved per column so that
  layers 1-3 and 4+ each reach their target; every layer below 1.49 m gets the
  deep value. Layers 8-10 (0.8-3.8 m) hold most of the water and change by
  about +-6% (5-95%, 2015-02-01).
- Soil temperature: layer 1 gets the surface shift, the thickness-weighted
  mean of layers 4-10 above 2.89 m gets the subsurface shift (linear in
  between), and the shift decays below 2.89 m with an e-folding depth of 3 m
  (`temp_decay_depth`; layer 11 gets 54%, layer 12 19%, layers 14-15 none). 3 m
  is about the damping depth of the annual temperature cycle,
  sqrt(2 kappa / omega) = 2.2-3.2 m for a soil thermal diffusivity of
  0.5-1 x 10^-6 m2 s-1.

Checked in the `hybrid_ic` restarts (soil and crop columns, against the `clim`
base):

| Date | Layers 8-10 (0.8-3.8 m): \|dT\| > 3 K | 99th percentile \|dT\| | Shift crosses 0 C with the layer's ice unchanged | Layer 11 (3.8-6.3 m) \|dT\| > 3 K |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2015-02-01 | 3.2% of columns (26S-79N) | 5.1-5.4 K | 1.9-3.5% | 0.8% (63-81N) |
| 2015-08-01 | 0.8-2.3% | 2.9-3.8 K | 0.8-1.4% | 0.2% |
| 2026-02-01 | 5.6-5.7% | 6.5 K | 3.5-4.6% | 1.6% (63-78N) |

Open points for refining the approach:

- The deep water and temperature anomalies are the 7-289 cm (`swvl2-4`,
  `stl2-4`) anomaly extended down, so they assume the deep anomaly has the same
  sign and phase as the root zone. Deep anomalies usually lag and damp the
  surface ones, and ERA5-Land's deep layers are themselves model-derived
  (HTESSEL) rather than observed.
- The temperature shift does not repartition liquid and ice: where it crosses
  0 C in a layer holding ice (mostly permafrost and seasonally frozen soil, the
  table above), ELM will melt or freeze the layer in its first steps and the
  latent heat will pull the temperature back toward the base.
- Layer 10 (2.3-3.8 m) gets the full deep shift, although it extends below
  2.89 m, and the water table (`ZWT`, `WA`) is not adjusted to the changed
  deep water.
- The `z_max`, ratio bounds, envelope, ramp depth (1.49 m), and decay depth are
  choices of this workflow, not taken from the literature; the 2015 evaluation
  in notebooks 03 and 04 is the test of them.

`20251024_s2d_spinup` prefix so the hindcast driver can use them through
`my_refcase2`.

Cost: per calendar month, reading the model key fields takes about 3.5
minutes and the ERA5 fields about 2 minutes; both are cached under the
output root (`initial_conditions/cache/`, about 1.5 GB per month) and reused
for the 2015 and 2026 dates of that month. Writing copies one 14.6 GB ELM restart per date and branch.

`v3.LR.amip_0101.HICCUP.atm_era5.EN00-EN09.eam.i.YYYY-MM-DD-00000.nc` (the
hindcast driver's `my_refcase1`) and `atm.json` to `YYYYMMDD/atm/`; like the

| Source | When | Members |
| --- | --- | --- |

The reconstruction reproduces the BruteForce 2015-02-01 EN00 bit-for-bit
(`cprnc`). Tang's 2026-02-01, 05-01 and 08-01 HICCUP files arrived on
2026-10-15; they have the same dimensions and variables as the 2015 files, but
the non-ERA5 fields (aerosols, trace gases) come from a 2005-2014 monthly
climatology of `v3.LR.amip_bonus_0101` `eam.i` files instead of the restart of
the same date. 2026-11-01 is pending until its file exists. A date whose
`atm.json` exists is reported as done and skipped (`overwrite_existing=true`
redoes it), so rerunning the script only builds the new dates.

```bash
CHECK_ONLY=true ./make_atm_ic.bash   # preflight: source per date, pending dates, protected outputs
./make_atm_ic.bash                   # runs here on an interactive node or in a job, submits from a login node
```

reanalysis-based files named by calendar date (YYYYMMDD): the ocean from

| --- | --- | --- |
| `make_ocn_ic.bash` | CFSR ocean state (`mpas_YYYYMMDD.cfsr.ic.nc`: temperature, salinity, layer thickness, land-ice fields; velocities are zero, as in the BruteForce CFSRv2 restarts) appended onto the BruteForce `mpaso` restart of `template_year` (default 2018) on the same calendar date, which supplies only the fields CFSR does not have (flux accumulators, SSH history, frazil) | `ocn/20251024_s2d_spinup.mpaso.rst.YYYY-MM-DD_00000.nc`, `ocn/rpointer.ocn`, `ocn/ocean.json` |
| `make_ice_ic.bash` | NSIDCv6 sea ice (`YYYYMMDD/mpassi.IcoswISC30E3r5.iceArea_from_NSIDCv6_spunup.YYYYMMDD.nc`), an alternative to each method's sea ice; the preflight checks the mesh and restart variables against the BruteForce `mpassi` restart | `ice/nsidc/20251024_s2d_spinup.mpassi.rst.YYYY-MM-DD_00000.nc`, `ice/nsidc/rpointer.ice`, `ice/nsidc/ice.json` |

Both keep the source time as `xtime.orig`, so the model starts from the
rpointer date (as in `../BruteForce/luke_cfsr/`), check every input and the
mesh (and, for the sea ice, the restart variables) before writing, and share
helpers in `util/ic_lib.sh`. A hindcast takes the atmosphere from `atm/`, the ocean
from `ocn/`, the land, river, and coupler from `lnd/`, `rof/`, and `cpl/` of
one method, and the sea ice from `ice/<method>/` or `ice/nsidc/`. Usage (edit the USER SETTINGS block first; the ocean takes about 1
minute and 4 GB per date):

```bash
CHECK_ONLY=true ./make_ocn_ic.bash   # preflight: inputs, mesh, protected outputs
./make_ocn_ic.bash                   # runs here on an interactive node or in a job, submits from a login node
./make_ocn_ic.bash --run             # run here anywhere (or execution_mode="run" in the script)
CHECK_ONLY=true ./make_ice_ic.bash   # likewise for the sea ice
./make_ice_ic.bash
```

**Sea-ice files not yet used** (2026-10-26). The IcoswISC30E3r5 files pass
the preflight (mesh and variables), but their ice state does not vary with the
date: every date has NH ice area 14.0 and SH 0.03 million km2 and NH ice
volume 3.3 thousand km3. The BruteForce restarts have NH/SH area 14.0/4.7
(2015-02-01) and 5.6/16.4 (2015-08-01) million km2 and NH volume 14-24
thousand km3. The earlier EC30to60E2r2 files show the same pattern, so this is
in the source data, not the remapping. Every file is time-stamped
`0001-01-07_00:00:00`, and on every date the NH holds a winter-like ice cover (the whole Arctic, Hudson Bay,
Okhotsk, Baltic) while the SH is almost ice-free (`05_reanalysis_seaice_check.ipynb`). `ice/nsidc/` is not
written until this is resolved with the file provider.

## 2015 sensitivity test

Initial conditions from another year differ from the actual 2015 BruteForce
restarts, so 2015 is used to measure how much this matters. For each 2015
date there are four sets of initial conditions:

| Variant | Sea ice, land, river | Location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual (control) | 2015 restarts from `20251024_s2d_spinup` | `/global/cfs/cdirs/e3sm/zhan391/v3.LR.S2D.ENSINT/BruteForce/2015-MM-01-00000/` (existing) |
| `clim` | Year closest to the same-date climatology (2015 excluded) | `/global/cfs/cdirs/e3sm/S2S2D/RTENSO2026/initial_conditions/2015MM01/<comp>/clim/` |
| `obs` | Year closest to the 2015 observed (ERA5-Land/NSIDC) anomaly (2015 excluded) | `/global/cfs/cdirs/e3sm/S2S2D/RTENSO2026/initial_conditions/2015MM01/<comp>/obs/` |
| `hybrid_ic` | `clim` year + 2015 observed anomaly | `/global/cfs/cdirs/e3sm/S2S2D/RTENSO2026/initial_conditions/2015MM01/<comp>/hybrid_ic/` |

Comparing hindcasts from these shows the forecast sensitivity to using a
selected year instead of the actual sea-ice, land, and river states. Because
the actual 2015 restarts exist, the evaluation sections of 01-03 also score
every candidate year and each initial condition against them, showing how
close each method is to the true 2015 state.
